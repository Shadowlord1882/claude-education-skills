# Subject adaptations

The default structure (multiple choice, short theory, calculations, data and experiments) is built for sciences. Keep the same idea for other subjects: a quick-recall section, a section of explained or applied answers, and a section where the student works with *evidence* (data, a source, a text), because that is where understanding shows. Keep the 50-mark, 60-minute default unless the user says otherwise.

## Sciences (biology, chemistry, physics, combined science)

Use the default structure. Experiment contexts that work well:

- Biology: pulse rate and exercise, food tests, enzyme or temperature investigations, field sampling.
- Chemistry: solubility curves, chromatography (Rf comparison), reactivity of metals, temperature change in reactions.
- Physics: distance/time graphs, speed from timings, moments, pressure and area, refraction angles, spring extension.

Typical equations to list for lower grades: speed = distance ÷ time, moment = force × distance, pressure = force ÷ area, concentration = mass ÷ volume, density = mass ÷ volume.

## Mathematics

| Section | Type | Marks (of 50) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Multiple choice / quick fluency | 10 |
| B | Short structured problems | 16 |
| C | Multi-step problem solving and word problems | 14 |
| D | Data handling: tables, charts, averages, graphs, or investigation/pattern tasks | 10 |

Marks are for method as well as answer, so the mark scheme uses M and A marks and allows error carried forward. Choose numbers so every answer is checkable; compute answer keys in code.

## English and languages

| Section | Type | Marks (of 50) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Reading comprehension on an **original** short passage (write it yourself, 200–350 words at grade level) | 20 |
| B | Vocabulary, grammar and sentence work | 10 |
| C | Short writing (paragraph, email, description) with a simple criteria-based scheme | 20 |

Do not quote copyrighted texts at length; use an original passage. Writing tasks are marked against criteria (content, organisation, accuracy, style), so give the mark scheme as banded descriptors with an example of what a top-band answer includes.

## History, geography, social studies

| Section | Type | Marks (of 50) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Multiple choice / short recall | 10 |
| B | Short explanations (causes, effects, features) | 14 |
| C | Evidence work: a short described source, data table, climate or population data, or a map described in words | 16 |
| D | One extended answer (6–8 marks) marked with levels | 10 |

Level-marked answers need a mark scheme with a point list and level descriptors. Avoid questions that depend on an image you cannot supply; describe the evidence in text or a table instead.

## Computing and ICT

Multiple choice and short theory (10 + 14), tracing or writing short code or pseudocode (14), and a data or scenario question such as spreadsheet formulas, logic tables or binary conversions (12). Verify code and conversions by running them.

## Anything else

Map the subject onto the three-part pattern above: quick recall, applied explanation, work with evidence. If the user states a board whose papers have a known format, follow that format and say which parts you matched.
